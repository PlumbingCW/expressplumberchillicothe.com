Water Heater Installation in Chillicothe, OH.

A planned water heater installation gives you time to choose the right unit and have it set up correctly. Chillicothe Express Plumbing installs gas, electric and hybrid water heaters in Chillicothe, Ohio for new homes, remodels, additions and upgrades. Call (866) 310-6010 to schedule a visit.

Planning ahead matters because an install involves a gas line, a vent or an electric circuit, and the water connections. Each piece has to be sized and routed for the unit you choose.

What a new installation includes.

A proper install is more than swapping a tank. It includes shutoff valves on the cold supply, flexible connectors or hard piping, a temperature and pressure relief valve with a full-size drain line, a drain pan where a leak would do damage and a vent or electrical connection to match the unit.

On gas units we also install a sediment trap and a gas shutoff, and we check the flue draw after firing. A thermal expansion tank is added where the plumbing is a closed system, for example when a pressure-reducing valve or check valve sits on the supply.

We take photos of the finished job so you have a record.

New construction and additions.

In a new build or an addition, the water heater should be planned with the plumbing layout. Placing it near the bathrooms and kitchen shortens hot-water wait times and saves energy.

We coordinate with the builder on rough-in locations, venting paths and electrical, so the unit fits without changes later. See also our plumbing installation page for fixtures and rough-in.

Relocating or upgrading an existing heater.

Moving a heater to open floor space or to cut the distance to the bathrooms is possible. It needs new pipe runs, gas or electric, and a vent path. The cost depends on the distance and the walls involved.

Upgrading from a 30 to a 50 gallon tank is a common request when the family grows. Make sure the closet or alcove can fit the larger unit.

Codes, permits and the Ohio license.

Ohio plumbing contractors are licensed through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board under Chapter 4740 of the Revised Code. Plumbing inspections are the jurisdiction of the county health district under Ohio Administrative Code 1301:3-2-04.

Whether a particular swap needs a permit depends on the local rule and the scope. We tell you at the quote stage so there are no surprises on installation day.

Installs in Ross County homes.

Water in the Chillicothe area comes from the Teays Valley Aquifer, and hardness can build scale in a new heater. We recommend a flush each year and an anode check every few years.

If your house sits low near the Scioto or Paint Creek and has a history of basement water, we mount the heater on a stand and pan it, so a flood does not damage the new unit.

What affects installation pricing.

A clear price comes before any work.

  • Unit and efficiency. Larger or higher-efficiency units cost more.
  • Venting and fuel work. New flues, gas lines or circuits add scope.
  • Code items. Expansion tanks, pans and valves.
  • Disposal of the old unit. Removal and haul-away can be included.

Ask your utility and the manufacturer about rebates on efficient models. Keep receipts and the data plate photo for warranty and tax purposes.

Code points on a new heater.

A new heater follows the manufacturer's manual and the Ohio Plumbing Code, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), for the relief valve, discharge pipe and expansion control. Gas units add the NFPA 54 National Fuel Gas Code for venting and combustion air. Federal efficiency rules from the U.S. Department of Energy set the minimum efficiency of what you can buy.

What to do before we arrive for an install.

A little preparation makes the job faster and safer.

  • Clear a path to the old heater and about three feet of working space around it.
  • Shut off the gas at the appliance valve or the breaker for an electric unit.
  • Close the cold water supply valve and leave the nearest hot tap open.
  • Move stored items and anything flammable away from the heater area.
  • Tell us if you smell gas, and leave the house and call 911 or your gas supplier first.
