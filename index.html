Chillicothe: a river city with a plumbing history.

Chillicothe was founded in 1796 and served as Ohio's first and third capital. The city sits between the Scioto River and Paint Creek, 45 miles south of Columbus. That setting shapes plumbing here: low ground near the rivers, older homes with older pipes and cold winters that test every exposed line.

The 1913 flood put about 70 percent of Chillicothe under water, and levees along the Scioto now protect the city. Basements near the rivers still see groundwater, which is why sump pump repair and basement waterproofing are part of what we do.

Our neighbors include the retail corridor on North Bridge Street, Yoctangee Park downtown, Adena Regional Medical Center and the Hopewell Culture National Historical Park on the city's north end. Restaurants and shops along those roads call us about grease lines and restroom repairs through our commercial plumbing service.

Water in Chillicothe: what comes out of your tap.

The city's water comes from groundwater in the Teays Valley Aquifer, pumped at a treatment plant that has been running since 1991. Groundwater carries minerals, and over time they show up as scale in water heaters and on fixtures.

Customers outside the city who buy from the Ross County Water Company have a published 2024 hardness of 120 to 140 milligrams per liter. If you are not sure where your water falls, test your tap. Our water softener and filtration page explains what a test result means.
