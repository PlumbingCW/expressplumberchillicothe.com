Plumber in Downtown Chillicothe, OH: Old Residential District.

Downtown Chillicothe mixes some of the oldest houses in Ohio with brick buildings that hold apartments above shops. If a drain backs up or a water heater fails in the Old Residential District, call (888) 910-8644 and describe the building.

Where the Old Residential District sits.

The National Register listing for Chillicothe's Old Residential District was added on November 28, 1973. It describes the district as roughly bounded by 4th, South Mulberry, South Walnut and 7th Streets, and counts 41 contributing buildings.

Chillicothe was surveyed in 1796, and the Society of Architectural Historians' Archipedia notes that many early buildings survived the Great Fire of 1852. Federal style dominates the early houses, with later revival styles and Queen Anne examples added through the nineteenth century. Brick construction is common.

The district is not a fenced-off museum. People live here, run businesses here and rent apartments here, and that mix is what shapes the plumbing calls we talk through.

Apartments over shops and shared lines.

Chillicothe's comprehensive plan describes the downtown district as a grid of short blocks with two and three story buildings, often with shops or restaurants on the ground floor and offices or apartments above. It also notes recent reinvestment along Paint Street.

In a building like that, one water shutoff or one drain stack can serve several units. Before you call, find out who owns the line and where the shutoff is. A tenant can describe the symptom, and the owner or manager usually decides on the repair.

When the problem is in a shared line, tell us how many units are affected and which floors. That tells us whether the trouble is at one fixture or somewhere further along.

Older houses and what a camera can tell you.

A house from the 1800s has usually been re-plumbed in pieces over the decades. We do not assume what is inside any particular wall. Pipe materials vary from house to house, and the only reliable answer comes from looking.

For drains, that means a camera. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what the picture shows and when it is worth paying for before you dig or relocate a line. For supply pipes, a plumber can often identify the material at the meter, the shutoff and under sinks.

If you own an older building and are thinking about a bigger project, read about repiping first. Repiping is a decision to make with evidence, not a guess based on the building's age.

City water and sewer, and when to call them.

Inside Chillicothe, water and sewer service comes from the City of Chillicothe Water & Sewer department, which handles water distribution, sewer collection and billing. If water is leaking in the street or a city main is involved, the city is the right call.

Everything on your side of the connection is private. That includes the pipe from the meter to the building and the sewer lateral to the main. When you call us, we help work out which side of the line the problem is on, and we say so if it belongs to the city.

Rules and sources for this page.

Plumbing work in Ohio follows the Ohio Plumbing Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and plumbing contractors are licensed under Chapter 4740 of the Revised Code. Plumbing inspections fall to the county health district under Ohio Administrative Code 1301:3-2-04, although certified municipal building departments may employ plumbing inspectors.

The district facts on this page come from the National Park Service's National Register listing (NRIS 73001523), the Society of Architectural Historians' Archipedia and the City of Chillicothe's comprehensive plan.
