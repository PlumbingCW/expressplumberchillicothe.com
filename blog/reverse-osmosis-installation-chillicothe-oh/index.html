Reverse Osmosis System Installation for Chillicothe, OH Homes.

A reverse osmosis system is the most thorough filter you can put under a kitchen sink. It pushes water through a membrane that holds back dissolved minerals and many contaminants. Whether you need one depends on your water, and the first step is to read your water quality report.

This guide explains how a system works, what the Ross County Water Company's 2024 report says and what an installation involves.

How a reverse osmosis system works.

01

Sediment filter.

Water first passes through a filter that catches sand and rust particles.

02

Carbon filters.

One or two carbon stages remove chlorine and organic compounds that affect taste and protect the membrane.

03

The membrane.

Under pressure, water passes through a very fine membrane. Dissolved solids stay behind and wash to the drain.

04

Storage tank.

Filtered water collects in a small tank under the sink.

05

Post filter and faucet.

A final carbon stage polishes the taste before water reaches a separate drinking faucet.

Do you need one.

Start with the report. If your water already tastes fine and meets standards, an RO unit improves the taste and reduces dissolved solids but is not necessary for safety. If you have a specific concern, such as lead in an old house's plumbing, a lab test tells you more than a guess.

Many people choose RO for coffee, ice and cooking because it removes the taste of minerals. It is a preference as much as a need.

The ongoing cost is filters and membrane replacement, and the water sent to drain.

Installation: what the plumber does.

The unit mounts under the kitchen sink, and a small dedicated faucet is installed at the sink deck, which may need a hole drilled in the sink or the counter. A feed line connects to the cold supply, a drain saddle clamps to the sink drain above the trap and a tubing line runs to the storage tank.

We pressure-test the connections, flush the new filters and check the tank pressure. The first tank should be discarded. Our faucet repair page covers the sink-side work if your existing faucet needs attention at the same time.

A refrigerator ice maker can be connected to the RO line, which means another run of tubing.

Upkeep: filters, membrane and sanitizing.

Sediment and carbon filters usually need replacing about once a year. Membranes last longer, often two to five years, depending on water. Replacing them on schedule keeps the taste and protects the membrane.

A yearly sanitizing of the system prevents the buildup of bacteria in the tank. We note the date on the unit and set a reminder.

Choosing a system.

Look for a certified system with a rating for the contaminants you care about. Look at the cost of replacement filters, since a cheap system with expensive filters costs more over time.

Tankless RO units are compact and make water on demand, at the cost of higher price and a need for power. Standard tank systems are simple and reliable.

What a reverse osmosis system costs to run.

Beyond the install price, the cost is filters and water. A typical household replaces the sediment and carbon filters yearly and the membrane every few years. Each replacement set is a modest purchase, but it recurs.

RO also sends some water to the drain while it makes drinking water. For a drinking and cooking tap that is a small amount over a year, but it is worth knowing if your water bill is already a concern.

Mineral taste and remineralizing filters.

Water from an RO unit can taste flat because the minerals are gone. Some systems add a final cartridge that returns a small amount of calcium and magnesium. If you dislike the taste, ask about one at the time of install.

Where the system fits in the cabinet.

Most systems need a space about the size of a few paper-towel rolls plus room for a tank. A sink with a disposal and a dishwasher hookup crowds the cabinet, so measure before you buy.

If space is tight, a compact tankless unit or a system with a remote tank in the basement can solve it. Mention the cabinet layout when you call so we bring the right parts.

Testing the finished system.

After the install we let the tank fill, drain it once and check the pressure. We look at the drain saddle for leaks and test the faucet for steady flow. You should hear the system make water for a few minutes after each use, and a quiet gurgle at the drain is normal.

What the Ross County Water Company report says.

The Ross County Water Company's 2024 annual water quality report describes groundwater from eight production wells in the Teays River Aquifer. After treatment it lists total hardness of 120 to 140 milligrams per liter, alkalinity of 240 to 280, iron below 0.10 and manganese below 0.03. Its lead 90th percentile was 1.5 parts per billion against an action level of 15, and its copper 90th percentile was 0.94 parts per million against an action level of 1.3.

Those are the company's own published figures for its system, which serves rural Ross County customers including the Village of Kingston's supply. They do not describe your house's pipes. City of Chillicothe customers should read their utility's current report. If you are near Kingston or on a private well, a certified lab test tells you what you actually drink.
