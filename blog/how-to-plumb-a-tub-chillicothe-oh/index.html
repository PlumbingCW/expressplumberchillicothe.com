How to Plumb a Tub in Chillicothe, OH: Drain, Vent and Valve.

Plumbing a tub comes down to three systems: the drain that carries water away, the vent that lets the drain breathe and the valve and spout that deliver hot and cold water. Each has a rule that decides whether it works or fails in a year.

This guide explains how a tub is plumbed, in the order the work is done, and where the rules around permits and licenses come in.

The drain: slope, trap and overflow.

The drain pipe leaves the tub through a shoe at the bottom and connects to a trap, usually a P-trap, that holds a small plug of water. The trap blocks sewer gas from rising. From the trap the drain runs to the main drain with a downward slope.

The overflow is a second opening high on the tub that connects to the drain above the trap. It prevents spills when the tub is full. The overflow gasket is a common leak point, since it sits behind the wall and cannot be seen.

The tub drain is typically one and a half inches. A steady slope of about a quarter inch per foot moves water without leaving solids behind.

The vent: why a drain needs air.

Without a vent, water leaving the drain pulls a vacuum behind it and slows down or siphons the trap. A vent pipe connects to the drain near the trap and rises to a stack that goes through the roof.

Venting rules set maximum distances between the trap and the vent. Getting these wrong is the most common reason a new bathroom gurgles. In a remodel, moving a tub often means moving the vent.

If a tub drains slowly and nearby fixtures gurgle when it empties, the vent may be blocked by a nest or ice. A plumber checks the roof termination.

Order of work for a new tub.

01

Set the rough-in.

Position the drain and valve at the right heights and distances from the tub, based on the model you chose.

02

Connect the drain and vent.

Run the drain pipe with the correct slope and tie in the vent.

03

Install the valve and supply lines.

Set the valve body on a bracket at the specified depth and connect hot and cold supply.

04

Pressure test and leak test.

Test supply lines under pressure and fill the drain with water to check for leaks before closing the wall.

05

Set the tub and finish.

Install the tub, connect the shoe and overflow, then set the trim and spout.

The valve: pressure balance and cartridges.

A pressure-balancing valve keeps the temperature steady if someone flushes a toilet. It uses a cartridge that wears over time and collects scale. Replacing a cartridge is a short repair if the valve has an access panel.

Install the valve where a repair is possible without tearing out tile. A removable panel in the closet or the next room saves a great deal of money later. See our faucet repair page for cartridge replacement.

DIY or hire a plumber.

A homeowner can swap a tub spout or a cartridge. Replacing a tub with a new one means opening the wall, adjusting the drain and vent and usually working with a permit. Mistakes in the drain are expensive because they sit under the floor and behind finished walls.

A plumber will also pressure test the supply and make sure the overflow gasket is sealed. Our plumbing installation page describes how a remodel visit works.

Mistakes that cause the most call-backs.

The most common mistakes are a drain with too little slope, a trap that is too far from the vent and a valve set at the wrong depth so the trim will not fit. All three are hidden once the wall closes.

A leaking overflow gasket is next. It is hard to check after the tub is set, so test it with water before you close the access.

Finally, skipping an access panel for the valve turns a ten-minute cartridge swap into a tile repair.

Choosing a tub material and what it means for plumbing.

Acrylic and fiberglass tubs flex and need solid support under the floor. Cast iron is heavy and needs a strong floor. Steel tubs ring and need insulation.

The material affects how the drain connects and how the tub sits. Check the manufacturer's rough-in sheet before the plumber sets the drain position.

Code rules for a tub drain.

Tub drains follow the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in Ohio, which sets the trap, the drain slope and the venting that keeps the trap from siphoning. Plumbing inspection belongs to the county health district under Ohio Administrative Code 1301:3-2-04, so ask whether a permit is needed before you open a wall.

Tub plumbing, permits and who inspects it in Ohio.

Ohio licenses plumbing contractors through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board under Chapter 4740 of the Revised Code. Under Ohio Administrative Code 1301:3-2-04, plumbing inspections are the jurisdiction of the county health district, and certified municipal building departments may employ plumbing inspectors.

The City of Chillicothe's building department issues residential building permits and contractor registrations and enforces its residential building code. Whether your tub project needs a permit depends on scope, so confirm with the city or the county health district before you begin. A bathroom in a village such as Kingston may fall under different offices, which is why we ask the address first.
