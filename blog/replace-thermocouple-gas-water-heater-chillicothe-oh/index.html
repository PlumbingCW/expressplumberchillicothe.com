Replacing a Thermocouple on a Gas Water Heater in Chillicothe, OH.

A gas water heater that loses its pilot every few days usually has a weak thermocouple. The part is inexpensive, the job is short and the same symptoms can also come from a dirty pilot or a bad gas control valve, so it pays to test before you buy.

This guide explains what the part does, how to check it and where the line sits between a careful homeowner project and a plumber's job.

What a thermocouple actually does.

A thermocouple is a small rod of two dissimilar metals that sits in the pilot flame. Heat from the pilot makes it produce a tiny electrical current. That current holds the gas valve open.

If the pilot goes out, the current stops, and the valve closes and cuts off the gas. It is a safety device. A weak or worn thermocouple fails to hold the valve open even when the pilot is lit, so the pilot dies as soon as you release the button.

That symptom, a pilot that lights but will not stay lit, is the classic sign.

Signs the thermocouple is the problem.

These point toward the part rather than the whole unit.

  • The pilot lights while you hold the button and goes out when you release it..
  • The pilot burns weak or has a yellow, flickering tip..
  • The tip of the thermocouple is black with soot or visibly bent..
  • The heater has run for years with no pilot trouble, then suddenly fails..
  • There is a smell of gas. If so, stop. See the safety section below..

Safety first: gas smells and who to call.

If you smell gas near the heater, do not touch light switches or try to relight anything. Leave the house, then call 911 or your gas supplier from outside. Once the gas is shut off and checked, call a plumber.

Your gas supplier's name is on your monthly bill. Chillicothe-area homes are served by different suppliers depending on the street, so we point you to your bill rather than guess.

How the replacement works.

01

Turn the control to off and let it cool.

Close the gas control knob and wait several minutes. Shut the gas at the line if you can.

02

Open the burner access door.

Remove the access cover to reach the pilot assembly and the control valve.

03

Unscrew the old thermocouple.

It connects to the control valve with a small nut. The other end sits in a bracket at the pilot flame.

04

Fit the new part.

Insert it into the same bracket so the tip sits in the flame. Tighten the nut snugly, not hard.

05

Relight and check.

Follow the label on the heater to relight, then watch the pilot for several minutes and test the burner.

Where a plumber should take over.

Replacing a thermocouple is a modest repair on a heater in good condition. It becomes a plumber's job when the unit is old, the burner is dirty, the flue draws poorly or the gas valve is suspect. Those problems are not obvious from the outside.

A plumber also checks combustion. If the burner flame is yellow, if soot is building on the bottom of the tank or if the flue is warm only at the draft hood, the heater is not breathing right. Our water heater repair visit includes those checks.

If the tank itself is old and rusted at the base, a new thermocouple only buys time. See water heater replacement for the next step.

What a thermocouple costs.

The part is inexpensive at a hardware or plumbing supply counter. What costs more is the visit, which covers the diagnosis, the combustion check and cleanup of soot or rust.

We give a clear price before work begins, and we say honestly when a part this small is not the real cause.

Keeping the pilot area clean.

Dust and lint collect around the pilot and the burner, especially in basements that share space with a dryer. A dirty pilot burns weak and heats the thermocouple poorly.

A plumber cleans the burner area during a visit. Between visits, keep the area around the heater clear of stored items and never place flammable liquids nearby.

Standards for gas water heaters.

Gas storage water heaters are built to ANSI Z21.10.1, and the gas piping and venting around them follow the NFPA 54 National Fuel Gas Code. The thermocouple is a listed safety part, so use the exact replacement the manufacturer specifies. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or your gas supplier.
