Steam or hot water: which system do you have.

A steam boiler heats water to steam, which rises through pipes to radiators, and returns as condensate. It has a sight glass showing water level. A hot water boiler circulates heated water through pipes to radiators, baseboards or in-floor loops. It has a pressure gauge, not a sight glass.

Many older Chillicothe houses have one or the other, and they fail differently. The first thing we ask on the phone is which you have.

Leaks and water level problems.

Boiler leaks are often at a gasket, a circulator flange or a relief valve. These are repairable. A leak from the boiler body, particularly at a section joint on a cast iron boiler, usually means replacement.

On a steam boiler, low water can damage the unit. Check the sight glass. If it is empty, turn off the burner and call.

If you add water often, the system is leaking somewhere. A dry boiler room does not mean there is no leak, since leaks can be in the walls or buried in a slab.

Maintenance before the cold season.

Boilers do best with an annual check before winter. A steam boiler needs its low water cutoff flushed. A hot water boiler needs its expansion tank and air vents checked.

A boiler that runs without issues for years can stop on the coldest night. A fall check is cheaper than an emergency call.

Repair or replace.

A boiler can last decades, and many in older homes do. When parts are scarce, efficiency is poor or the heat exchanger leaks, replacement is the better value.

We give you a clear comparison, including the cost of keeping the old one running.

Boilers in older Ross County homes.

Boilers are common in older homes in Chillicothe, particularly those with radiators. Winters in southern Ohio are cold enough to test them for months. Pipes in unheated basements, garages and crawlspaces freeze, and a frozen boiler line can burst.

If your boiler room is in a basement that has flooded in the past, tell us. Controls and burners that have been under water need replacement.

Standards behind boiler work.

Boilers follow the manufacturer's service instructions and the NFPA 54 National Fuel Gas Code for gas piping and venting. Pressure relief valves are a safety device and are not adjusted to chase a symptom. Ask any contractor which manufacturer instructions they are following for your model.

What to do before we arrive for a boiler problem.

Boilers combine gas, heat and pressure, so go slowly.

  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or your gas supplier before anything else.
  • Do not touch the relief valve or any pressure fitting.
  • Turn the thermostat down so the boiler stops calling for heat.
  • Note the pressure gauge reading and any error code, and read them to the person on the phone.
  • Keep the area around the boiler clear and dry.
