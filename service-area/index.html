How we decide which calls we can take.

We look at the address, the type of problem and whether service can be arranged. An emergency in the city usually gets the fastest arrival. Calls from farther out may take longer, and the person on the phone gives you an honest window when you call, never a guarantee.

For addresses outside the city, tell us the road name, whether you have a septic tank or city sewer and where your water comes from. Those answers decide which tools to plan for.

Water, sewer and housing differ by place.

Inside the city, most homes draw groundwater from the Teays Valley Aquifer through the municipal system. Around Kingston, the Ross County Water Company supplies water with a published 2024 hardness of 120 to 140 milligrams per liter. Private wells and septic tanks are common on county roads.

Those differences change which problems we expect. City houses see older drain lines and basement groundwater. Rural houses see frozen lines, pump trouble and septic-related backups. We plan the tools to match.

Housing age varies as much as water source. Chillicothe was founded in 1796, so its older blocks hold pipe from many eras, while newer subdivisions on the edge of town were built in later decades. We look at each house instead of assuming.

Emergency calls and planned work in the villages.

For a true emergency, tell the person on the phone and ask what can be arranged. For planned work such as a water heater swap or a repipe, we schedule a visit that suits you and list what we need to see in advance.

If you manage several properties across the county, tell us. We can keep your addresses and shutoff locations on file so the next call goes faster.

Call before you assume.

If you live on the edge of Ross County, or in a neighboring county, call anyway. We would rather tell you honestly whether we can reach you than leave you searching. If another plumber is closer for a true emergency, we will say so.
