Jammed disposal: what is safe to try.

With the power off, look under the unit for a small hex socket in the center of the bottom. A hex key turned back and forth frees the flywheel. From above, use a wooden spoon handle to push loose any visible object.

Once it turns freely, press the red reset button on the bottom and restore power. If it hums again or trips right away, the motor or the start capacitor is failing.

Do not use a screwdriver in the grind chamber or pour chemicals into a jammed unit. We see more injuries and damaged blades that way than from the jam itself.

Leaks under the sink.

A leak under the sink can come from the disposal, the dishwasher hose, the drain trap or the faucet supply lines. A towel and a flashlight help: dry each part, run water and watch where the first drop forms.

Leaks from the body of the disposal mean the housing has cracked, and the unit must be replaced. Leaks at the flange or the drain elbow are fixable with new seals.

If the cabinet floor is wet, remove everything stored there and dry it. Particleboard swells and rots quickly.

Kitchen sink clogs past the disposal.

When the sink fills with gray water, the blockage may sit in the kitchen branch past the disposal. Grease, coffee grounds and fibrous scraps such as celery and potato peel pack into the line.

We cable the line from the trap arm or a cleanout and, if grease is heavy, follow with a jet. More on the process is on our drain cleaning page.

A disposal that works fine but drains slowly is almost always a line problem, not a motor problem.

Choosing a replacement disposal.

Horsepower matters less than motor build quality and sound insulation. A half or three-quarter horsepower unit suits most households, and higher horsepower suits those who use the disposal heavily.

Continuous feed models start when you flip a switch. Batch feed models run only with a stopper in place, which some prefer for safety around children.

Septic households should ask us before installing one, because heavy food waste adds to tank solids.

Disposals and older Chillicothe kitchens.

Older houses near downtown Chillicothe may have kitchen drain lines of cast iron or galvanized steel that are rough inside, and a disposal pushes food scraps into them. Pipes like this clog more often and catch grease at every joint.

If your kitchen line has clogged repeatedly, a camera run before installing a new disposal can show whether the drain can handle one.

Electrical and plumbing rules.

A disposal ties into the drain trap under the Ohio Plumbing Code, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and into an electrical circuit that follows the National Electrical Code (NEC), NFPA 70. We shut off the circuit before touching the unit and never put a hand in the chamber. Use the manufacturer's hex key socket at the base to free a jam instead.
