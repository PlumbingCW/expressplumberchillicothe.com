Sewer backup: what to do first.

Do these in order while you wait for the plumber.

  • Stop running water in sinks, showers, washers and dishwashers..
  • Do not flush. Each flush adds water that has nowhere to go..
  • Turn off power to a flooded basement if you can reach the panel without standing in water..
  • Wear gloves and boots if you must enter, and wash well afterward..
  • Take photos of the water and any damaged items before cleanup..

What a failing sewer line looks like.

The sewer lateral is the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the municipal main or septic tank. When it fails, drains slow down together and rarely recover after a cable run. Floor drains gurgle, toilets burp and the yard may develop a soft or sunken area over the line.

The causes are age, tree roots, ground movement, offset joints and, in older pipe, collapse. Clay and cast iron from the early to mid twentieth century are the common culprits, and Chillicothe has plenty of both under older streets.

A new crack can turn into a blockage within weeks as roots follow the water in. Early action keeps the repair small.

Spot dig, pipe lining or full replacement.

The right method depends on the damage and what sits above the pipe.

Method.Best when.Considerations.
Spot repair (dig).One short break or offset, line otherwise sound.Needs excavation, but only at the failed section.
Pipe lining (trenchless).Cracks, root entry and light corrosion along a straight run.Minimal digging, limited if the pipe has collapsed or has sags.
Pipe bursting.Older pipe in poor shape along a long run.Needs entry and exit pits, replaces the pipe in place.
Full open-trench replacement.Collapsed or badly misaligned line.Most disruptive, but sometimes the only sound option.

Lining is not magic. If the pipe has a bellied section that holds water, lining it only locks in the belly. The camera tells us which case you have.

Roots, grease and what comes back.

Roots enter at joints and cracks because the wastewater carries moisture and nutrients. Cutting them clears the line but does nothing about the opening. Without a repair, they return in months.

Grease works the other way. It coats the pipe wall and narrows the opening until it blocks. A hydro jet removes the film, and a camera afterward shows whether the pipe is sound.

Flushing wipes marked flushable, paper towels and feminine products is a leading cause of blockages that start at a rough joint.

Septic homes outside the city.

Many homes outside the city limits, including in the rural townships near Kingston, use a septic tank instead of a municipal sewer. Backups there can come from a full tank, a blocked outlet filter or a failed drainfield rather than the line.

We check the lateral, but if the tank is the issue we say so. Septic pumping and drainfield work are not part of our service list, and we would rather tell you that on the phone than guess.

Sewer lines under a river city.

Chillicothe's sewers sit in ground that is often saturated, because the city lies between the Scioto and Paint Creek. High groundwater lets roots and silt find joints more easily. After heavy rain, backups may be tied to infiltration or high flows in the shared main rather than your own lateral.

A camera run tells the two apart. If the problem is in the public main, we document it with video and you can bring it to the city.

What affects sewer line repair pricing.

We quote after the camera, not before, because the footage drives the number.

  • Depth and length. Deeper or longer runs need more excavation or equipment.
  • What sits above. Driveways, trees, patios and utilities make digging harder.
  • Method. Lining, spot dig and replacement each carry very different prices.
  • Permits. Sewer connections may need a permit and inspection through the local authority.

Many homeowners policies exclude sewer backup unless an endorsement was added. Ask your insurer before the work, and save the camera footage, which shows the cause and the date.

Code and permit basics.

Sewer work between the house and the main or septic tank follows the Ohio Plumbing Code, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including slope, cleanout access and approved pipe. Under Ohio Administrative Code 1301:3-2-04 plumbing inspection belongs to the county health district, so a permit question goes there.
