What we install.

Our installation work covers toilets, sinks, vanities, tubs, showers, kitchen sinks, dishwashers, washing machine boxes, hose bibs, utility sinks and the water and drain lines that serve them. We also add shutoff valves, pressure regulators and relief valves.

We install fixtures you choose or advise on options. For some jobs, the cheapest fixture is not the cheapest to own, and we say so.

Rough-in and finish: two phases of a remodel.

Plumbing in a remodel happens in two visits.

Phase.What happens.Why it matters.
Rough-in.Supply and drain lines set in the open walls and floors.Must be done before drywall and inspected where required.
Finish.Fixtures set, connected and tested.Done after tile, paint and cabinets.

Moving a toilet or a shower drain is a bigger job than swapping a fixture, since the drain line slope and venting must be right. Ask early.

Bathroom plumbing.

A bathroom remodel may add a second sink, replace a tub with a shower or move the toilet. Each change affects the drain and vent. A shower valve and pan are set at rough-in, and the pan is tested before tile.

We install shower valves with accessible access panels where possible, so the next cartridge swap does not require breaking tile.

Kitchen and laundry plumbing.

A kitchen remodel often means a new sink, faucet, disposal, dishwasher connection and a filtered water line. The dishwasher drain needs a high loop or an air gap, and the disposal knockout must be removed correctly.

Laundry boxes need a standpipe at the correct height and a drain sized for the washer. A washer box with a leak can ruin the wall behind it, so we use quality valves and test them.

Permits and inspections.

Ohio plumbing contractors are licensed by the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board under Chapter 4740 of the Revised Code, and plumbing inspections are the jurisdiction of the county health district under OAC 1301:3-2-04. The City of Chillicothe's building department handles residential building permits and contractor registration.

Which permits apply to your job depends on scope and address. We tell you early in the quote.

Remodeling in a historic city.

Chillicothe has many historic houses and downtown buildings near the Ross County Courthouse. Old floors, thick plaster walls and existing cast iron drains shape what is possible. A new bathroom in a century-old house often means working around original framing.

If your home is in a historic area, check for design review rules before changing the exterior. Plumbing vents and wall penetrations are the likeliest to be affected.

What affects installation pricing.

A quote comes before we begin.

  • Scope. Swapping a fixture versus moving a drain.
  • Fixture quality. Valves, cartridges and finishes.
  • Access. Open framing versus finished walls.
  • Coordination. Waiting on trades adds visits.

Keep receipts, warranty cards and photos of the rough-in before the walls close. They help with resale and with insurance.

Permits and code for new fixtures.

New fixtures and added lines follow the Ohio Plumbing Code, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), including venting and trap rules. Plumbing inspection in Ohio belongs to the county health district under Ohio Administrative Code 1301:3-2-04. Ask whether your project needs a permit before work starts.
