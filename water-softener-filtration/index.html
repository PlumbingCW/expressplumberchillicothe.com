Water Softener and Filtration in Chillicothe, OH.

What the local numbers say.

The Ross County Water Company's 2024 water quality report lists total hardness of 120 to 140 milligrams per liter, about 7 to 8 grains per gallon, after treatment. It reports iron below 0.10 milligrams per liter and manganese below 0.03, and describes a groundwater source in the Teays River Aquifer.

That puts its water in the moderately hard range. City of Chillicothe water also comes from groundwater, and its customers should check their utility's current report or test their tap, since hardness differs from system to system.

Private wells in Ross County vary widely, and a lab test tells you more than any rule of thumb.

Softener repair.

Softeners fail in a few ways. The brine tank can bridge, with a crust that keeps salt from dissolving. The control valve can stop regenerating. The resin bed can wear out after 10 to 15 years. Water comes out hard, though the unit looks fine.

We test hardness before and after, check the salt level, the brine line and the valve. Most repairs are a clean-out, a valve rebuild or a new injector. A worn resin bed means replacement.

A softener that wastes salt or runs constantly is usually a control or seal problem.

Reverse osmosis at the kitchen sink.

An under-sink reverse osmosis unit forces water through a membrane, removing dissolved solids. It feeds a small dedicated faucet. Filters need changing about once a year, and the membrane every few years.

It suits drinking and cooking water. It does not soften the whole house. See also faucet repair if the existing tap needs work.

Softeners and water heaters.

Hard water shortens the life of tank and tankless water heaters by building scale. A softener protects them. If you have a tankless unit, a softener or an annual descale is part of keeping it healthy.

Water treatment questions in Ross County.

Chillicothe's municipal supply is groundwater from the Teays Valley Aquifer, treated at the city plant that came online in 1991. The rural customers around Kingston buy water from the Ross County Water Company.

If you have a private well, the county health district or a certified lab can test it. We are not a lab, and we do not claim to know your hardness without a test.

What affects treatment pricing.

We quote after we test.

  • System type and capacity. Larger households need larger units.
  • Water test results. Iron or manganese adds pre-treatment.
  • Plumbing changes. Bypass loops, drains and electrical outlets.
  • Maintenance plan. Salt, filters and membranes recur.

Treatment equipment rarely changes an insurance premium, but scale-related failures are not covered as sudden damage. Keep service records.

Standards for softeners and filters.

Home softeners are tested to the NSF/ANSI 44 standard, and reverse osmosis units to NSF/ANSI 58. Hardness is measured in grains per gallon, and a lab or test kit result decides whether you need treatment at all. The Ross County Water Company reports about 7 to 8 grains per gallon in its system.

What to do before we arrive for a softener or filter job.

A few checks save time on site.

  • Test your tap water hardness with a kit or ask the water company for its latest report.
  • Locate the main shutoff valve and the nearest floor drain or standpipe.
  • Clear the area around the main line and nearby outlet.
  • Note whether the water is municipal, well or from the Ross County Water Company.
  • Tell us if anyone in the home is on a sodium-restricted diet, because softening adds sodium.
