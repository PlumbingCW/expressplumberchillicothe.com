What the water service line is, and who owns it.

The service line is the pipe between the water main in the street and your house. The utility generally owns the line up to the meter and the homeowner owns it from the meter to the house, though the rule varies by system. Ask your utility to confirm where its responsibility ends.

Older lines are copper, galvanized steel or sometimes plastic, and they fail from corrosion, ground movement and age. A break between the meter and the house is the homeowner's repair.

Signs of a failing water line.

Watch for these in the house and the yard.

  • A wet or sunken patch in the yard along the line from the meter..
  • Water pressure that has dropped across the whole house, not just one fixture..
  • A meter that keeps running when all fixtures are off..
  • A higher water bill with no change in usage..
  • Muddy or discolored water after a repair or a main break..
  • Hissing or running sounds near the entry point..

Low water pressure: line or something else.

Low pressure in one fixture is a clogged aerator or cartridge. Low pressure in the whole house points at the line, the pressure regulator or a partly closed main valve.

We check pressure with a gauge at an outside spigot. A reading well below normal with the main fully open suggests a restricted or leaking line, a failing regulator or a utility issue. We ask your neighbors if they have noticed it too.

Old galvanized lines clog from the inside with rust, which also reduces flow. In that case, replacing the line is the fix. See also repiping for the plumbing inside the house.

Leaks in the meter box and at the shutoff.

Not every leak is in the line. A leaking valve at the meter box or a failing curb stop can leak water into the ground. Those belong to the utility if they are on its side of the meter, and we point you to them.

A homeowner-side leak at the connection is ours to repair. We tell you which side the leak is on before work starts.

Water lines in Ross County.

City customers in Chillicothe receive groundwater from the Teays Valley Aquifer, and rural customers around Kingston and the surrounding townships are served by the Ross County Water Company, which reports a distribution system without lead, galvanized-requiring-replacement or unknown-status service lines in its 2024 report. Your line from the meter to the house is your own, and its material depends on the year the house was built.

If you are not sure what your line is made of, we check at the entry point. Replacing an old galvanized line often improves both pressure and flow.

What affects water line repair pricing.

We quote before we dig.

  • Length and depth. Longer and deeper lines need more digging.
  • What sits on top. Driveways, sidewalks and landscaping increase restoration work.
  • Pipe material. Copper, PEX and HDPE differ in cost.
  • Hour of the call.Ask about any call-out charge before you agree to a visit.

A leaking underground line may be excluded from some homeowners policies, but others include service line coverage as an option. Check your policy, and keep the repair record.

Standards for the supply line.

Water service pipe has to be an approved material under the Ohio Plumbing Code, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Pipe and fittings for drinking water carry NSF/ANSI 61 marking. Where the line crosses from the water company's side to yours, the company's own rules decide who repairs what, so confirm that first.
