Plumber in Brewer Heights, Chillicothe, OH.

Brewer Heights is an established residential neighborhood north of downtown Chillicothe, with Chillicothe mailing addresses in ZIP code 45601. If a drain, water heater or sump pump needs attention, call (888) 910-8644 and give your street.

The homes are mostly mid-century in age and sit on generous lots. This page shares what public sources say and explains how to describe a problem so a call goes quickly.

Where Brewer Heights is.

A Ross County real estate directory describes Brewer Heights as an established residential neighborhood north of downtown, along the North Bridge Street corridor, with quick access to US 23, the Adena Health System campus and downtown. Nearby places it names include the Chillicothe Country Club, Yoctangee Park and the city pool.

The streets reported for the neighborhood are Club Drive, Fruit Hill Drive, Limestone Boulevard, Ridge Drive, Ridgeway Drive, Shawnee Drive, Tecumseh Drive and Turnberry Lane. Fruit Hill Drive and Limestone Boulevard have the most homes, and Turnberry Lane and Club Drive have a quieter, cul-de-sac feel.

Several of those streets also appear on the City of Chillicothe's ward map.

City or township? Check the parcel.

One directory lists Brewer Heights under Scioto Township, and the city's ward map shows several of its streets. Some parcels may therefore be inside the city and others outside it. We cannot tell from public pages which applies to your address.

It matters for two practical reasons. First, it decides whether the City of Chillicothe Water & Sewer department or another provider handles your public lines. Second, it decides who inspects permitted plumbing work. The Ross County Auditor's parcel record or your water bill tells you which applies.

Homes from the 1950s and 1960s: what to ask.

Mid-century houses have usually seen kitchen and bath updates over the years. We do not assume what any house contains. The material of the drain and supply pipes varies, and the honest way to find out is to look, with a camera for drains and with a visual check at the shutoff and under sinks.

Large lots can mean a long run between the house and the street. If drains slow down in more than one fixture, start with drain cleaning. If that keeps recurring, ask about a sewer camera inspection to see the line.

A water heater from the same decades as the house may have been replaced already. Check the label for the date. If the tank leaks, replacement is usually the answer. See water heater replacement.

Rules and sources for this page.

Permitted plumbing work in Ohio follows the Ohio Plumbing Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Ohio licenses plumbing contractors under Chapter 4740 of the Revised Code. Under Ohio Administrative Code 1301:3-2-04, plumbing inspection falls to the county health district. Before any digging for a sewer or water line, Ohio811 asks you to notify it at least two working days ahead.
